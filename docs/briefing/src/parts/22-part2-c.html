6  Horizon 1: from “runs on a laptop” to “safe to launch”

Horizon 1 Engineering and operations. Synthetic data only, throughout. Weeks 2–8

First, the three clocks

The most common way a founder loses a month is by treating every task as though it runs on their own clock. It does not. Sort the work by whose calendar it depends on, and start everything on somebody else's calendar in week one, even though it completes last.

OTHER PEOPLE'S CLOCK — start day 1, finishes weeks later ├─ trademark clearance §5, decision 1 ├─ counsel engagement + the brief §5, decision 3 ├─ independent security review ── book it now, run it week 6 └─ accessibility review with disabled users ── recruit now, run it week 7 YOUR CLOCK — start week 2, you control the pace ├─ production authentication ├─ connect the eight integrations that never ran ├─ encryption, scanning, backups └─ hosting, monitoring, on-call THE MARKET'S CLOCK — starts only after the launch gate in §7 └─ the first ten families §8
The single hardest rule in this document

No real person's memories enter the system until every box in §7 is ticked. Not a friendly beta family, not your own grandmother, not “just to test with something real”. The moment real material is in there, you have obligations you cannot yet meet, and the first archive is exactly the one you will care most about having handled badly. Synthetic data is not a limitation during Horizon 1; it is the point of Horizon 1.

Week What ships Why it is in this position
1 Start every external clock. Correct the pricing-page tense. Name the accountable person. These cost you hours and buy you weeks.
2–3 Production authentication. OIDC or passkeys, MFA enrolment, and password reset. Blocking item 4. Password reset is not on the blocking list but a pilot without it generates a support crisis in week two. VERIFIED
3 Real secrets. Replace every development placeholder and move them into a secret manager. SESSION_SECRET and STORAGE_SIGNING_SECRET currently hold strings that literally read dev-only-…-change-me. VERIFIED Shipping either is a total compromise of sessions or of object URLs.
3–4 Run the eight integrations that have never executed. Each is written and type-checked and unproven. Detailed below — this week also produces your real unit costs.
4–5 Encryption at rest with a customer-managed key; a real malware scanner. Blocking items 3 and 5. Today anyone with database or backup access reads everything, and the local scanner catches a test file and a mismatched type — it is not an antivirus. VERIFIED
5 Backups, and a restore you actually perform. Blocking item 6. Your own document says the recovery targets are stated, not measured. Restore into a clean environment and time it. VERIFIED
5–6 Hosting, deploy pipeline, monitoring, alerting, on-call. Instrument OpenTelemetry. It is configured and not instrumented, so today you would find out about an outage from a customer. VERIFIED
6 Independent security review runs. Blocking item 2. The isolation is currently tested by the person who wrote it.
7 Accessibility review with disabled users. Blocking item 7. Zero automated violations is a floor. Your users skew old; some will have low vision, tremor, or hearing loss. This is a core-audience issue, not a compliance checkbox.
8 Fix what the two reviews found. Run the §7 gate. Legal answers land. Reserve the week. Both reviews will find things.

A question worth asking before you spend on hosted composition

Your local composer is extractive: it can quote and stitch retrieved passages and structurally cannot originate a claim. That is a weaker system that reads more plainly — and it is also a system in which fabrication is unavailable rather than merely rare. Before you switch to a hosted model, put both in front of five real people and find out whether they prefer the fluent one. If they do not, you have just removed a cost line, a dependency and a cross-border transfer problem in one decision. INFERENCE
