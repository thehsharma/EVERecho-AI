13  The team, in hiring order

Five roles, each with the trigger that says it is time. Hiring before the trigger burns money you do not have; hiring after it means you have already been the bottleneck for a month.

Role Hire when Why this order
1 Interviewer / storyteller success You are conducting more than about six interviews a month, or interviews are being scheduled around your availability Interview quality is the product (§3, problem 5), and it is the first thing that breaks when you get busy. Hire for warmth and curiosity, not for technology — oral history, journalism, social work, chaplaincy and teaching all produce good candidates.
2 Engineer You are choosing between shipping and selling in the same week, more than once Everything is TypeScript with no build step and 279 passing tests, so a competent engineer is productive quickly. Have them start by taking the deployment and on-call, which is the work that most fragments a founder's attention.
3 Privacy and legal operations
fractional first 		A second jurisdiction, a first institutional contract, or the first data-subject request from someone who is not a customer The third-party-in-memories question (§5, brief item 4) will generate real operational work. Fractional is right until it isn't; the trigger for full-time is a second regulator.
4 Partnerships One channel has produced families twice, repeatably, without you personally in the room Hiring this before the channel is proven means paying someone to discover that your thesis was wrong — which you can discover yourself for free in §8 and §10.
5 Support lead Support minutes per archive × live archives exceeds what one person can absorb alongside another job See the warning below. This is not a normal support hire and staffing it like one will hurt someone.
Support here is not normal support

Your support inbox will contain a message from someone whose parent died last week asking why a recording will not play. It will contain families in conflict about who may see what. It will contain a person who wants to delete everything, at midnight, and means it.

Staff this deliberately: written escalation paths, a hard rule that nobody handles these alone, access to actual supervision, and a workload that assumes emotional labour rather than ticket throughput. And note the constraint you built — the support view shows operational metadata only, never memory content — which is right, and which means your support people will sometimes be helping with something they cannot see. Train for that explicitly. VERIFIED — scope is metadata-only in code

Who not to hire, and when not to

A growth marketer, earlymost of the standard playbook is closed to you (§11); they will be frustrated and you will waste the hire
A salesperson before the channel is provensee role 4's trigger
An AI/ML specialistthe retrieval and verification are done and deliberately conservative; there is no model problem to solve yet
A designer, full-time, earlythe interface is built and passes accessibility; use contract design for the asking artifact and the printed deliverable

The one thing you should not delegate

Conduct interviews yourself for longer than is comfortable — well past the first ten. It is the only place where you learn what the product must become, and it is the first thing every founder in your position hands off, because it does not feel like work on the company. It is the work on the company.
