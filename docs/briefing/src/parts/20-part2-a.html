5  Horizon 0: the decisions only you can make

Horizon 0 Nothing here is engineering. All of it blocks engineering. Weeks 0–2

Seven decisions. Two of them have hard deadlines imposed by outside parties and should start on day one because they take weeks of somebody else's time.

Decision 1 — The name

Your own README says EverEcho is “a working codename pending trademark clearance”. VERIFIED You cannot build a brand, buy a domain properly, print anything, or sign a channel partner on a name you may have to abandon. Every week you delay makes the eventual rename more expensive.

You did one thing very well here: the product name lives in configuration (PRODUCT_NAME), so renaming the software is a settings change, not a refactor. The expensive part is everything outside the repository.

Recommendation: start clearance on day one, in every jurisdiction you intend to operate in, in the relevant classes. If it is not clearly clearable within three weeks, rename now rather than hope. Budget this as the first professional-services money you spend.

Decision 2 — Where the company and the data live

Configuration currently says JURISDICTION=IN and DATA_REGION=local. VERIFIED Those were sensible development defaults and they are now a strategic choice with legal consequences: which law governs consent, where the database physically sits, whether using a US-hosted AI provider constitutes a cross-border transfer, and which regulator you answer to.

Recommendation: incorporate and host where your first ten families are, not where you imagine your hundred-thousandth will be. Keep the region a configuration value, as it already is, and treat a second region as a Horizon 3 problem. Do not architect for a geography you have no customers in.

Decision 3 — Engage counsel, with a written brief

This is the highest-leverage thing you will do in the next month, it is blocking item 1 on your own readiness list, and it runs on somebody else's calendar. Start it in week one. The brief is on the next page — do not send “please review our privacy policy”, send the questions.

Recommendation: find counsel with data-protection experience specifically, not a general commercial lawyer. Ask them directly whether they have advised on special-category data and on what happens to personal data when the subject dies. If they hesitate on the second question, keep looking.

Decision 4 — Name the accountable person

Blocking item 8: the incident runbook says “escalate” and there is no one to escalate to. VERIFIED If you are solo, the answer is you — but it must be written down, with a name, in the runbook, on the support page, and in whatever you sign with a customer. An accountability that is implied is an accountability that evaporates at exactly the wrong moment.

Recommendation: name yourself today, in writing, in INCIDENT_RUNBOOK.md, and commit it. Replace the name when you hire someone; never let the field be empty.

Decision 5 — Audio-first, or audio and video

From the storage arithmetic in §2: video can be one to two orders of magnitude larger than audio for the same hour, and you are promising preservation. This decision sets your cost base permanently and is very painful to reverse once families have uploaded video.

Recommendation: audio by default, video as an explicitly priced upgrade with its own storage term stated. Photographs and documents are cheap; treat them as included and encourage them, since they carry enormous emotional weight per megabyte.

Decision 6 — Provisional price

Your pricing page currently sets no price at all. VERIFIED You cannot run §8 without one, and §2 established the one thing you must not do: choose a cheap one.

Recommendation: pick a provisional number in week two using §9, and hold it for the first ten families. Do not discount to close — a discounted first cohort teaches you nothing about willingness to pay, which is the only thing that cohort exists to teach you.

Decision 7 — A stance, not a commitment, on money

Bootstrap or raise changes your hiring, your speed and how much of §6 you do yourself. §14 argues it properly. You need a provisional stance now because it determines whether you spend the next eight weeks building or talking.

Recommendation: assume bootstrap through §8. Ten paying families is the cheapest, fastest, most honest fundraising asset you can build, and it is worth more in a pitch than any deck you could write instead.
