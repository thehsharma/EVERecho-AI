18  The risk register

Ranked by expected damage, not by how alarming they sound. The first one is not a risk — it is a certainty you are not yet prepared for.

# Risk Likelihood Mitigation Early warning
1 A storyteller dies mid-engagement certain A written runbook, before the first family. See below. None. It simply happens.
2 A family conflict over access high The dispute-hold mechanism exists in the schema; write the human policy for who decides and on what evidence. Access requests from people the storyteller did not invite.
3 A third party objects to appearing in a memory medium-high Counsel brief item 4; likely needs a redaction path and an objection route in product. The first such email. Do not improvise a reply.
4 Cost to serve exceeds price medium-high Fill the §2 worksheet in Horizon 1; watch cohort margin monthly (§12). Cohort margin flat across three cohorts.
5 Founder is the single point of failure medium-high Hiring triggers in §13; document operations as you go; the runbook must be usable by someone else. You have not taken a full week off, and cannot.
6 A fabrication reaches a family and is believed low-medium Verification and third-person assertion already run on every answer; the abstention band in §12 is the alarm. Abstention rate falls without an obvious cause. Treat as an incident.
7 A breach low-medium The launch gate; encryption with a customer-managed key; independent review; forced isolation already in place. Anything anomalous in the audit trail. Read it weekly (§20).
8 A channel partner pressures a storyteller medium The partner-agreement rules in §10 — consent conversation without the partner present, declining is costless, no feedback on why. A rise in consent narrowing or revocation from one partner's families (§12).
9 Acquisition or investor pressure to remove the refusal medium Every instrument in §17. This is what they are for. The first time you hear yourself say “maybe just for enterprise”.
10 Provider dependency medium Every provider sits behind an adapter with a working local implementation — this risk is already largely engineered away. Keep it that way. A pricing change email from a speech-to-text vendor.
11 Reputational contamination with griefbots medium §4 and §11 — own the distinction loudly and early. Being described as one, in writing, by someone who read your site.
12 Regulatory action low, if §5 is done The counsel brief, honestly answered and implemented. A data-subject request you do not know how to answer.
Risk 1 in full: write this runbook before the first family

Your product is for older people, and you will be working with a cohort of them for months. Someone in your first fifty families will die during the engagement. This is not a risk to be mitigated; it is a scheduled event whose date is unknown.

What must be decided in advance, in writing, while it is still abstract:

  • How you find out, and who tells you.
  • What happens to a half-finished archive — recordings not yet transcribed, memories not yet approved. Nobody but the storyteller can approve them, and the storyteller is gone. Decide now what unapproved material means after death. This is the hardest question in the product and it has no default answer.
  • Who may act: the succession directive, if one exists, and what happens if it does not.
  • What you send the family, and when. Not a system email. A person.
  • Billing: suspended immediately, no renewal notice, no dunning. Check that your payment provider cannot send an automated reminder — this is the most likely way you cause real pain, and it is entirely preventable.
  • Who on your team is told, and how they are supported.

Rehearse it once, out loud, before you need it. It is a grim hour and it is the single highest-value hour of preparation available to this company. INFERENCE
