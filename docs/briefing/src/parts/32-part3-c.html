11  Growth without breaking your own rules

Most growth playbooks are unavailable to you, and not by accident — you removed them on purpose. This section says exactly what is closed, what remains, and why what remains is unusually strong.

What is closed to you

Standard tactic Why you cannot use it
Content-based targeting and retargeting Memory content may never reach analytics. The constraint is enforced in the code that builds each event, not left to discipline. VERIFIED
Engagement email, digests, streaks, reminders There is no notification scheduler, deliberately. Reminders about a parent's mortality become guilt with almost no effort. VERIFIED
Rich behavioural analytics from third-party SDKs Most consumer analytics tooling assumes it may see page content. Yours may not. Expect to build a small amount of first-party measurement instead.
Testimonials quoting real memories Possible, but only under a separate, explicit, revocable marketing consent that is never a condition of service and never bundled into the main flow. Treat every such quote as borrowed, not owned.
Referral mechanics with social pressure “Invite your siblings before it's too late” is precisely the guilt loop the product prohibits.
Urgency and scarcity marketing The honest version of urgency here is a person's death. Do not go near it.

What remains — and it is better than what you gave up

1. Give away the questions

The interview question set is your most valuable and least protectable asset, and §3 established that interview quality is the actual product. Publish it: a free, beautiful, genuinely useful guide to interviewing your own parents, with no product requirement at all. Most people who download it will do nothing, some will try and discover how hard it is to do well, and a few will come back and pay you to do it properly. It costs nothing, it is honest, it demonstrates expertise better than any claim, and it is the single best top-of-funnel asset available to this business. INFERENCE

2. Publish the readiness document

As argued in §4. Radical transparency about your own gaps is both a trust asset and a story journalists will actually run, because almost nobody does it.

3. Be the contrasting quote

Stories about AI resurrection products appear regularly, and every one needs someone credible to say what the responsible version looks like. That person can be you, and it costs you nothing but availability. Write the position clearly once, keep it current, and be easy to reach.

4. The asking artifact

From §3, problem 2 — the letter or page a child gives a parent. It is a product feature and a growth mechanism at once, because it travels between exactly the two people who must both agree. It does not exist yet. Build it in Horizon 2.

5. The physical deliverable

The printed biography draft is a marketing object that sits on a table and gets picked up by visitors. In a category with a delayed verdict (§3, problem 5), a tangible artefact is how you get word of mouth a decade before the archive is truly needed.

6. Informational search

People search for how to record a parent's stories, what to ask a grandparent, how to preserve old photographs. Those searches are the closest thing this category has to declared intent, and answering them well is a durable asset that does not require you to say anything untrue.

The pattern

Every channel that remains open to you works by being genuinely useful before anyone pays, rather than by manufacturing pressure. That is slower. It is also the only kind of growth that is consistent with the product, and consistency here is not a nicety — the moment your marketing behaves differently from your product, the trust that is your entire differentiator is gone, and it does not come back.

12  The metrics that matter

Fewer than you think, and several of them are unusual. Two of the standard ones will actively mislead you if you track them.

The five that decide whether this works

Metric Definition Why
Time to first approved memory Payment → the storyteller approving their first memory Your true activation event. Everything before it is a promise; this is the moment the product has done something. Directly measures §3, problem 2.
Archive completeness Decades, relationships and themes covered versus a target shape The only early proxy for a verdict that otherwise arrives years late (§3, problem 5). Turns an unmeasurable outcome into a visible gap.
Members per archive People with active access Your real retention driver. Each additional member makes cancelling less thinkable.
Gross margin, per cohort Revenue minus cost to serve, by month of joining The alarm for the §2 margin trap. If cohort margin is not improving as you automate, concierge has quietly become the company.
Twelve-month retention Archives still paying after a year The only retention number that means anything for a custody product. Measure it annually; do not infer it from monthly activity.

Two that are specific to what you built

Metric What it tells you
Abstention rate
questions answered with the refusal sentence 		A health metric with a band, not a target. Too low and something is answering things it should not — check verification. Too high and either the archive is too thin (a completeness problem, tell the family) or retrieval has broken (an engineering problem). Alert on movement in either direction; a sudden drop is the most dangerous signal this system can produce.
