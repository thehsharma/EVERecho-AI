Building the business

Price, channel, growth, measurement and team — the five things that turn ten families into a company.

9  Pricing and the business model

Section 2 established the constraint: a cheap price forecloses the outcome. This section turns that into a structure you can put on a page.

Price the occasion, not the software

Cost-plus pricing will produce a small number, because your marginal costs are genuinely low. Do not price that way. Ask instead what a family already spends at this moment in their lives — a professional photographer for a milestone celebration, an hour with an estate lawyer, a headstone, a funeral. Those are the anchors your buyer is actually holding, and they are all far above what a software subscription would suggest. INFERENCE

The structure I would use

Component Shape What it is for
Capture One-time, large The guided interviews, the transcription, the structuring, the first biography draft, and the handholding. This is where the real work and the real cost sit, and where the value is most legible to the buyer.
Preservation Annual, modest Storage, backups, keeping it reachable, keeping it exportable. Recurring because the obligation is recurring.
Family access Included, unlimited Never charge per seat. Every additional family member increases the value, deepens retention (§3, problem 4) and costs almost nothing. Charging for them is the single most self-defeating pricing choice available to you.
Additional chapters One-time, smaller Years later, more sessions. Offered only when the family asks — never prompted, per §3.
Long-term preservation Optional prepay A stated term paid up front. Attractive to buyers, and dangerous to you — see the warning below.

As a starting heuristic, not a market observation: set capture in the band a family would pay a good professional for a day's work plus a deliverable, and set annual preservation at roughly a tenth to a fifth of that. That shape puts you in model C of §2 without any heroic assumptions. ASSUMPTION — validate in §8, cohort of ten

Before you sell any prepaid preservation term

A one-time payment for long-term storage is revenue today and an obligation for decades. Three things must be true first: counsel has answered whether it creates trust, escrow or reserve obligations (§5, brief item 11); you have real storage costs from Horizon 1; and the term is stated in years, not described as “forever”. Never write the word forever on a pricing page. You cannot promise it, and the one thing this company cannot afford is a promise it cannot keep.

Decide the non-payment policy now, and publish it

A family will stop paying. Perhaps they cannot afford it; perhaps the storyteller has died and nobody feels able to make the decision. What happens to the archive is a question you must answer before anyone pays, in plain words, on the pricing page.

Decision — what lapse means

The options are: delete after notice; freeze and retain read-only; retain fully at your cost; or export-and-close.

Recommendation: a long grace period, then export-and-close — you generate the full export, deliver it, confirm receipt, and then delete on a stated schedule. It keeps your storage liability bounded, it holds nothing hostage, and it is consistent with everything else the product promises. Your shutdown-and-portability commitments already point this way; make it the individual-account policy too.
