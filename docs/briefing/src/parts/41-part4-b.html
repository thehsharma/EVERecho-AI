16  The staged path to scale

Five stages. Each has one thing that must become true before the next is attempted, and a branch point where the honest answer might be to change direction rather than push harder.

Stage Archives Roughly What must become true Branch if it doesn't
0 10 Month 5 People pay full price and storytellers reach a first approved memory Reprice, or fix activation. Do not proceed on hope.
1 100 Year 1 One channel produces families repeatably without you in the room. Capture is partly productised. Cohort margin is rising. Try the second channel in §10. If two fail, the channel thesis is wrong and consumer-direct must be tested properly.
2 1,000 Year 2–3 Gross margin approaching software rather than services. Interviewers are hired and trained, not you. Twelve-month retention measured and acceptable. The real branch point. If margin is stuck, this is a services business — a good one, but not the §2 outcome. Decide deliberately whether that is acceptable.
3 10,000 Year 4–5 Multiple channels. Institutional accounts (model D) contributing. Operations boring enough to survive a bad week. If consumer stalls but institutions pull, follow the pull — that is the §4 infrastructure fork opening legitimately.

Sustaining roughly four-fold growth for six consecutive years is the shape of companies that reach this outcome. It is also where most attempts stop — typically between stages 2 and 3, when the founder-led magic stops working and the machine that replaces it has not been built. INFERENCE

The stage-2 to stage-3 transition is the whole game

Everything before stage 2 you can do through effort and taste. Everything after it requires that the quality of an interview does not depend on you being in the room. That is a training, tooling and standards problem, and it is the one to start solving at stage 1 — a year before you need it — because founders who wait until they feel the pain take eighteen months to recover from it.

Two honest observations about this table

First: stages 0 and 1 are things I would bet on. Stage 2 is genuinely uncertain and depends on a margin question nobody can answer from here. Stages 3 and 4 are arithmetic, not forecasts — they describe what would have to happen, not what will.

Second: stopping at stage 2 or 3 is not failure. A company with a thousand or ten thousand families, sound margins and an intact promise is a good outcome by any measure that is not this document's title. Keep that in view, because the arithmetic in §2 has a way of making a real business feel like a disappointment, and that feeling has ended more good companies than any competitor.
