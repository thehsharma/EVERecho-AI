10  The channel question

Section 3 concluded that you cannot manufacture the occasion, so you must attach to people who already own it. This section says which people, in what order, and how to test one without committing to all of them.

What makes a channel good, for this product specifically

Owns the momentis already present at a trigger event
Holds the trustthe family already takes their advice
Cannot touch simulationyour refusal is a requirement, not a quirk
Reaches the storyteller, not only the buyersolves §3, problem 2
Has a budget or a fee relationshipcan transact without a new purchase decision

The candidates, ranked

Channel Why it fits The problem with it
1 Estate, will and private-client advisers Present at exactly the moment a person is thinking about what they leave behind. High-trust, fee-based, used to handling sensitive family material. Clients skew to the right age and means. Slow, relationship-driven sales. They will not risk their client relationship on an unproven vendor — which is precisely what §4's readiness document is for.
2 Senior living communities Concentrated population, an activities budget, staff whose job is meaningful engagement, and a recurring institutional relationship rather than one family at a time. Institutional procurement. Consent gets complicated when a facility introduces the service — the storyteller must still be the one who decides, and you must design so that it visibly is.
3 Hospice and palliative care The most acute version of the need; legacy work is already part of good practice. Deeply values the refusal — no hospice can be near a simulation product. The hardest possible operating conditions: little time, fragile participants, and a cohort where death during the engagement is not a risk but an expectation. Do not start here. Earn it.
4 Diaspora and community organisations An unusually strong version of the need — the generation that migrated is ageing, and the reason they left is the first thing lost. Trusted community intermediaries; natural word of mouth. Fragmented, often unfunded, and the buyer may be the community rather than the family. Test as a source of families, not as a revenue channel.
5 Archives, museums, universities Oral-history practice already exists, with real methodology you can learn from. Budget for provenance and rights management. Values citation natively. This is a different product with a different buyer, and it pulls you toward §4's infrastructure fork. Genuinely promising; a distraction before month twelve.
6 Direct consumer advertising Fast to start. Low intent, split incentive, no occasion, delayed verdict. Every structural problem in §3 is at its worst here. Not recommended before you have a proven channel.
Decision — which one to test first

Recommendation: estate and private-client advisers, one partner, during §8. They combine trust, an owned occasion, a transacting relationship and a client base that can pay a model‑C price. Senior living is the strong second and a better fit if your first ten families come through one. Hospice is where this product may matter most and it is the last one you should attempt, not the first — go there when your operations are boring and your death runbook has been used.

How to test a channel without building a channel programme

  1. One partner. One. Five partners produce five weak relationships and no signal.
  2. Ask for introductions, not a contract. Three or four families is a complete test. Revenue share and paperwork can wait until you know it works.
  3. Give them the readiness document unprompted. Their real question is what happens to their reputation if this goes wrong. Answering it before they ask is the fastest trust you will ever buy.
  4. Watch who actually does the asking. If the adviser will not raise it with a client, the channel is dead however enthusiastic they were in the meeting. Enthusiasm is not distribution.
  5. Instrument it: introductions made, families who took a call, families who paid, families who reached a first approved memory. Four numbers. If introductions are high and calls are low, your asking artifact is weak. If calls are high and payment is low, your price or your proof is wrong.
The consent trap in every institutional channel

When a facility, an adviser or a hospice introduces the service, there is a structural risk that the storyteller feels the decision has already been made for them by someone with authority over their care or their money. Your product refuses to proceed without them — but refusing is not enough if the person feels obliged.

Design against it explicitly: the storyteller's consent conversation happens without the introducing party present, declining is unremarkable and costs the family nothing, and the partner is never told why an archive did not proceed. Write this into the partner agreement. It will lose you a small amount of revenue and it is not negotiable — a consent obtained under institutional pressure is the exact failure this entire product exists to prevent. INFERENCE
