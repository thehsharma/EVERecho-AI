3  What EverEcho is not

This list is the specification. Each item is refused somewhere in the code, and each refusal has a test that fails if somebody removes it.

ProhibitedWhere the refusal actually lives
Voice cloningThe consent document requires voiceAndLikeness.cloning === false; any policy that sets it true is refused at compile time with a named reason, before schema validation runs.
Face cloning, avatars, lip‑syncSame gate. There is no generation adapter, no model interface and no storage column for a synthesised likeness.
First‑person persona chatassertThirdPerson() runs on every composed answer before it is returned. A response containing first-person voice attributed to the subject is discarded, not rewritten.
Posthumous simulationConsent mode perform exists in the type so it can be named and refused; it is hard-disabled and every path that reaches it denies.
Consciousness or continuity claimsNo product surface makes one. The composer is extractive — it can only quote and stitch retrieved passages, so it cannot originate a claim of any kind.
Automatic death or incapacity transitionsNo inactivity timer changes an archive's lifecycle state. A succession directive requires a named steward and an explicit, recorded human action.
Scraping private accountsThere is no social ingestion path. Material enters only by upload or by a recorded interview session.

Advertising on memories; selling dataNo ad surface, no third-party analytics, no data-sharing adapter exists to be enabled.

On the word "prohibited"

In most products, a list like this is a policy page. Here it is a set of branches that return DENY with a named reason code, a set of CHECK constraints, and a set of assertions in the answer pipeline. The difference matters because policy pages are edited by anyone, and authorize() is edited by someone who has to make 145 unit tests pass afterwards.

4  Why the boundary is the product

The obvious commercial instinct is to treat the prohibitions as a launch position — ship the safe version, add the voice clone in v2 when the market asks. That instinct is wrong here for a reason that is structural rather than moral.

The boundary is what makes the archive trustworthy to the person filling it

The storyteller is being asked to hand over the most private material they have: what they regretted, who they fell out with, what they were afraid of. They will do this only if they believe the material will be used the way they were told. A product that will one day simulate them is a product they must reason about differently today — and the reasoning ends with them telling the safe stories, the ones already told at every wedding. The archive fills up with anecdotes and none of the truth.

The boundary is what makes the answers useful to the family

An answer that might be fabricated is worth nothing, because the family cannot tell which ones are. The moment the system is permitted to generate plausible content, every previous answer becomes retroactively suspect. Citation is not a trust feature bolted on the side; it is the only thing that makes the output mean anything.

The boundary is the defensible position

Anyone can build a griefbot — the components are commodity and the differentiation lasts one quarter. What is genuinely difficult to build, and therefore difficult to copy, is the machinery underneath the boundary: a consent model granular enough to be honest, an authorisation engine that cannot be bypassed, a retrieval path that respects both, a verification step that drops unsupported sentences, and a deletion system that actually deletes. That is eight months of unglamorous work, and it is the whole moat.

The one-sentence version

EverEcho refuses to simulate people not because simulation is difficult, but because refusing is the only way the archive can be worth filling.

What the refusal costs, stated plainly

It costs the demo. A voice clone demonstrates in fifteen seconds; a cited, abstaining, third-person archive takes ten minutes to explain and its most impressive behaviour is declining to answer. This is a real go-to-market problem and it is not solved by this build. It is named here because a pitch that hides its hardest problem is not a pitch, it is an advertisement.
